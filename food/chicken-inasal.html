Marinade Ingredients

🍗
Chicken
1 whole (~1.5 kg) or 4–6 leg quarters
Leg quarters (thigh + drumstick) are traditional — they stay juicy over charcoal heat. Score the flesh deeply so marinade penetrates.
🧪
Cane Vinegar
2 cups (sukang maasim)
The backbone of inasal. Sukang iloko or sukang maasim give the authentic tang. Tenderises the meat and keeps it moist on the grill.
🌿
Tanglad (Lemongrass)
8 stalks
Bruise each stalk with the back of a knife to release its oils. Use the white/pale parts and some of the green. Provides the signature citrus-herbal aroma.
🫚
Fresh Ginger
3-inch knob, peeled & sliced thin
Pound or grate for stronger flavour, slice for subtler heat. Adds warmth and cuts through the fat of the chicken skin.
🧄
Garlic
6–8 cloves, minced
Essential base of the marinade — don't skip. Pound with salt to a paste for more even distribution throughout the meat.
⚠ Not in your list
🍋
Calamansi
8–10 pieces, juiced (or ¼ cup lime juice)
The citrus element critical to authentic Bacolod inasal. Brightens the vinegar base and adds a floral acidity calamansi juice uniquely provides.
⚠ Not in your list
🧂
Salt
1 tablespoon
Seasons the marinade and draws moisture into the meat. Use sea salt or rock salt if available.
🍬
Sugar
1 tablespoon
Balances the acidity of the vinegar and calamansi. Also helps the skin caramelise and char slightly on the grill, adding colour and depth.
🫙
Black Pepper
1 tablespoon
Freshly cracked is best. Adds a subtle heat to the marinade. Some recipes use ground white pepper for a milder, more earthy spice.

Basting Oil

🍳
Chicken Oil
½ cup (rendered from chicken skin/fat)
Render chicken skin trimmings in a pan over low heat until golden. Strain off the crisps. This is what you baste onto the chicken while grilling — never before.
🟠
Annatto / Atsuete Seeds
2 tablespoons
Infused into the hot chicken oil while it renders, giving inasal its iconic orange-red colour. Without atsuete, the chicken won't have the characteristic Bacolod hue.
⚠ Not in your list
Things to add to your shopping list
  • Garlic (6–8 cloves) — a core flavour in the marinade, not optional for authentic inasal.
  • Calamansi (8–10 pieces) — or substitute ¼ cup fresh lime juice. This is the citrus note that defines the dish and balances the vinegar.
  • Annatto / Atsuete seeds (2 tbsp) — infused into your chicken oil, this gives inasal its signature orange colour. Without it the basting oil will still taste great but won't look the part.
  • The chicken itself — 1 whole chicken cut into pieces, or 4–6 leg quarters. Leg quarters are most traditional and hold up best over charcoal.
How to make the chicken basting oil
Collect the skin trimmings and any excess fat when you butcher the chicken. Place them in a cold pan and set over low heat — you want the fat to render slowly, not fry. Add 2 tbsp annatto/atsuete seeds directly to the pan. As the fat renders, the seeds will release their deep orange colour into the oil. This takes about 10–15 minutes. Once the skin scraps are golden and crisp, strain out both the scraps and the seeds. What remains is your orange-tinted chicken oil. Keep it warm on the side of the grill. You baste the chicken with this oil only during the last half of cooking — too early and the sugars in the marinade will burn before the chicken is done.

Method

  1. 01
    Prepare the chicken
    If using a whole chicken, cut into quarters or pieces. Score the flesh deeply with a knife — 2–3 cuts per side going almost to the bone. This helps the marinade penetrate and ensures even cooking. Pat dry with paper towels.
    ⏱ 10 min
  2. 02
    Make the marinade
    Bruise the 8 lemongrass stalks by pressing firmly with a rolling pin or the flat of a knife. In a large bowl combine: cane vinegar, bruised lemongrass, thinly sliced ginger, minced garlic, calamansi juice, salt, sugar, and pepper. Stir until the salt and sugar dissolve.
    ⏱ 5 min
  3. 03
    Marinate the chicken
    Add the chicken pieces to the marinade, turning to coat well and pressing it into the scored cuts. Cover and refrigerate for a minimum of 4 hours — overnight (8–12 hours) gives the best flavour. Turn the chicken at least once midway through.
    ⏱ 4–12 hrs
  4. 04
    Render the chicken oil
    While the grill heats up, render the chicken oil with annatto seeds over low heat (see box above). This takes 10–15 minutes. Strain and keep warm. This is your basting oil — prepare it before you start grilling so it's ready when you need it.
    ⏱ 15 min
  5. 05
    Grill over charcoal
    Prepare a charcoal grill for medium heat — you want consistent, even heat, not a raging fire. Remove the chicken from the marinade and let excess drip off. Grill skin-side down first. Turn every 5–7 minutes to prevent burning. Total time: 30–40 minutes depending on piece size. The chicken is done when juices run clear and internal temp reaches 74°C (165°F).
    🔥 35 min grill time
  6. 06
    Baste with chicken oil in the final stretch
    Only start basting with the annatto chicken oil once the chicken is mostly cooked through — roughly the last 10–12 minutes. Brush generously on both sides with each turn. The oil will give the skin a beautiful orange-red sheen and a rich, slightly smoky flavour. Do not baste early or the sugar in the marinade combined with the oil will char and burn.
    🔥 Last 10–12 min only

Tips & notes

Charcoal is non-negotiable Gas grills work in a pinch but the smoky char from charcoal is what makes inasal taste like inasal. Use hardwood charcoal for longer, more even heat.
Don't rush the marinade 4 hours is the bare minimum. Overnight is ideal. The vinegar needs time to work into the scored flesh — this is what makes the chicken tender all the way through, not just on the surface.
Baste late, not early The sugar in the marinade already makes the skin prone to burning. Add the chicken oil only in the last 10–12 minutes. This is the most common inasal mistake.
No calamansi? Use lime Calamansi is the authentic choice, but fresh lime juice is a very close substitute. Avoid bottled lemon juice — the flavour profile is too different.
Serve with garlic rice Authentic inasal is served with sinangag (garlic fried rice), a small dish of the leftover chicken oil for dipping, and a saucer of sukang maasim with sliced chilli.
Atsuete substitute If you can't find annatto seeds, use ½ tsp annatto powder or paprika dissolved in the chicken oil for colour — though the flavour won't be quite the same.
